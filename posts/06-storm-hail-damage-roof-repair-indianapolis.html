The first 24 hours after storm damage

  1. Stay safe. Don't climb onto a wet or damaged roof, and stay clear of downed lines.
  2. Stop the water. Move valuables, set out buckets, and drain a bulging ceiling stain into a bucket through a small hole.
  3. Photograph everything: ceilings, the attic if it's safe, the roof from the ground, dented gutters, damaged siding and debris. Write down the date and time of the storm.
  4. Get the roof secured. An emergency tarp or temporary repair prevents more interior damage. Keep the receipt.
  5. Get an inspection with photos from a local roofer so you know what's damaged before you call your insurer.
  6. Call your insurance company and ask about your deductible, how roof age is handled, and when their adjuster can inspect.

What size hail damages a roof?

It isn't just golf-ball hail. In a 2025 study, the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety (IBHS) found that:

  • Concentrations of hailstones under one inch can significantly reduce a roof's resistance to future large hail.
  • Cumulative granule loss from repeated small impacts was greater than the loss from a single 2-inch hailstone.
  • Shingles exposed to weathering and small hail were about ten times more susceptible to damage in a later severe hailstorm.

So if your area had a hailstorm, even a "small" one, have the roof checked. NRCA also recommends an inspection after any severe weather.

Emergency roof repair: what to expect

When you call an emergency roofing company in Indianapolis, the visit should include:

  • Securing the roof with a tarp or temporary patch over the damaged area
  • Clearing debris such as limbs and loose shingles
  • Documenting damage with photos for your insurance claim
  • Planning the permanent repair or replacement once your claim and the weather allow

Under Indiana law, if you approve emergency or temporary work in writing to prevent damage to your home, the contractor can be paid a reasonable amount for that work even if you later cancel the main contract.

Filing a roof hail damage insurance claim in Indiana: your rights

RuleWhat it means for you
3-business-day cancellation (IC 24-5-11-10.6)For contracts signed after June 30, 2017, you can cancel before midnight of the third business day after the later of signing or receiving your insurer's written final coverage decision. You must get a Notice of Cancellation form with the contract.
RefundIf you cancel, the contractor must return your payment or deposit within about ten days. The statute says ten days; the required form says ten business days.
No deductible rebates (IC 24-5-11-10.5)Contractors may not offer to pay or rebate any part of your deductible, or offer gifts or credits to get you to sign.
Contractors aren't public adjustersA roofing contractor can't act as your public adjuster under Indiana law. A roofer can document damage and meet your adjuster, but negotiating your claim is your job or a licensed public adjuster's.

How to get insurance to look at your roof fairly

For any storm damage roof insurance claim:

  • File promptly. Policies have reporting deadlines.
  • Have your own roofer's photos and written scope ready before the adjuster visits.
  • Ask your roofer to be present at the adjuster inspection.
  • Get the adjuster's estimate in writing and compare it line by line with your roofer's scope.
  • Don't sign a contract that commits you before your insurer decides. Indiana's cancellation rule protects you, but reading first is better.

Spotting storm chasers

A real storm damage roofing contractor in Indianapolis has a local address, local reviews and no pressure tactics. Watch for:

  • Out-of-state plates and no local address
  • "We'll cover your deductible" (illegal in Indiana)
  • Pressure to sign today, before your insurer inspects
  • Large cash deposits before any work
  • No Notice of Cancellation form with the contract
