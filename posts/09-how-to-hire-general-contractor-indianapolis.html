Short answer

To hire a general contractor in Indianapolis: get two or three detailed written quotes for the same scope, check Google and BBB reviews, look at finished projects like yours, confirm who will run the job day to day and who handles permits, and sign only a contract that meets Indiana's requirements under IC 24-5-11-10: a detailed description of the work, start and completion dates, the price, and the contractor's name, address and email.

Whether you're planning home remodeling in Indianapolis, adding a room or rebuilding after storm damage, the contractor you choose matters more than almost any material decision. This checklist covers what a general contractor does, the questions to ask, the red flags, and what Indiana law says your contract has to include.

What a general contractor does

  • Prices the full scope and puts it in writing
  • Schedules and supervises every trade: framing, roofing, plumbing, electrical, drywall, finish
  • Orders materials so the job doesn't stall
  • Coordinates permits and inspections
  • Keeps the site safe and clean
  • Is the one person accountable for the result

10 questions to ask a general contractor

  1. Can I see finished projects like mine, and talk to those homeowners?
  2. Who will be on site every day, and who do I call with questions?
  3. Which work does your own crew do, and what's subcontracted?
  4. Who pulls the permits and schedules inspections?
  5. What exactly is included, and what are allowances?
  6. What's the payment schedule, and which milestones trigger payments?
  7. What are the approximate start and completion dates, and what could change them?
  8. How do you handle change orders?
  9. Can I see proof of insurance?
  10. What happens if something goes wrong after the job is done?

What Indiana requires in your contract

Under Indiana Code 24-5-11-10, a real property improvement contract must include at least:

  • Your name and the address of the property
  • The contractor's name, address and an email address
  • Contact information for whoever handles customer problems
  • The date the contract was submitted and any deadline to accept it
  • A reasonably detailed description of the work, with specifications provided before work starts if they aren't included
  • Approximate start and completion dates, and contingencies that could change them
  • The contract price
  • Whether subcontractors, vendors or others will supply labor or materials
  • Signature lines for the contractor and every consumer party

For storm-damage work paid by insurance, Indiana also gives you a right to cancel within three business days of your insurer's final coverage decision (IC 24-5-11-10.6) and bans contractors from offering to pay your deductible (IC 24-5-11-10.5). See our storm damage guide.

Red flags

  • No written contract, or a one-line "remodel kitchen" scope
  • Pressure to sign today, or a large cash payment up front
  • No local address, or reviews you can't find anywhere but their own site
  • "We don't need a permit for that" on structural, plumbing or electrical work
  • A price far below the others with no explanation of what's missing

Comparing quotes from general contractors in Indianapolis

Line the quotes up item by item: demolition, framing, roofing, windows, plumbing, electrical, drywall, finishes, permits and cleanup. Big price gaps usually come from items left out or carried as low allowances. A good contractor will walk you through every line.
