Short answer

Timing is the biggest clue. If the ceiling stain grows during or after rain, especially near an outside wall or under a chimney, skylight or vent, it's most likely a roof or flashing leak. If it shows up after showers, flushing or running water, it's plumbing. If it happens only in winter, suspect ice dams or condensation in a bathroom exhaust duct. Stop the water, photograph it, and call the right pro.

A water stain on the ceiling, a brown ring, or a drip from a light fixture is stressful, especially when you don't know where it's coming from. Water travels along rafters, pipes and drywall before it shows up, so the stain is often not directly under the source. Here's how to trace a water leak in the ceiling to its source and get the right ceiling leak repair.

What to do right now

  1. Protect your things. Move furniture and electronics, and put down a bucket and towels.
  2. Drain a bulging stain. Poke a small hole in the center of a sagging bubble so water drains into the bucket instead of bringing the drywall down.
  3. Watch for electrical hazards. If water is near a light fixture, turn off that circuit.
  4. Shut off the water if the leak seems tied to plumbing.
  5. Photograph the ceiling, the attic if safe, and the roof from the ground.

Simple tests you can do

  • Wait for rain. If the stain grows only with rain, it's the roof.
  • Run each fixture upstairs one at a time and watch the stain.
  • Check the water meter with every fixture off. If it moves, a supply line is leaking.
  • Look in the attic with a flashlight after rain for wet decking, stained rafters or daylight.

Fixing the ceiling after the leak

Painting over a stain only works once the source is fixed and the area is completely dry. Wet or sagging drywall should be cut out and replaced. Before painting, seal the area with a stain-blocking primer so the ring doesn't bleed through.
