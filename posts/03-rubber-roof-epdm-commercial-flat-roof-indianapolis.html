Short answer

A rubber (EPDM) roof can be repaired when the problem is local: a puncture, an open seam or failed flashing on a membrane that's still flexible and dry underneath. Replace it, or recover it where code allows, when leaks keep returning in different places, the membrane is shrinking or brittle, or the insulation below is wet. Properly installed EPDM routinely lasts at least 38 years, according to the EPDM Roofing Association.

Flat and low-slope roofs don't use shingles. They need a continuous membrane, and rubber (EPDM) is one of the most common. If you own a commercial building in Indianapolis or Avon, or a home with a flat porch, garage or addition roof, this guide covers what EPDM is, why flat roofs leak, and how to decide between rubber roof repair, recover and replacement.

What is EPDM rubber roofing?

EPDM stands for ethylene propylene diene monomer, a synthetic rubber sold in large sheets. It's adhered or fastened to the roof deck over insulation, the seams are sealed, and it's flashed at every wall, curb, drain and pipe. It's widely used for:

  • Commercial, retail and multi-family buildings
  • Porch, sunroom and garage roofs
  • Low-slope sections of home additions

In March 2026, the EPDM Roofing Association reported that properly installed and maintained EPDM roofs routinely deliver at least 38 years of service life, based on a 2025 survey of roofing professionals.

Common flat roof and rubber roof problems

Most flat roof repair calls come down to one of these:

  • Open seams where the adhesive or seam tape has failed
  • Flashing failures at parapet walls, curbs, rooftop units and pipes
  • Punctures from foot traffic, dropped tools or storm debris
  • Shrinkage that pulls the membrane away from edges and corners
  • Ponding water from clogged drains or sagging decking
  • Wet insulation under the membrane, which hides a leak and keeps spreading

Choosing a commercial roofing contractor in Indianapolis

A commercial roof leak can close a store, damage inventory or upset tenants. Good commercial roof repair in Indianapolis starts with finding where water actually enters. When you're comparing rubber roofing contractors or commercial roofers, ask:

  1. Will you find the source, not just the drip? Water travels under a membrane, so the leak is rarely right above the stain.
  2. Will you check for wet insulation? Repairs over wet insulation fail.
  3. What will you photograph and report? Property managers need documentation.
  4. How will you protect tenants and operations while work is under way?
  5. What maintenance do you recommend? NRCA recommends inspections at least twice a year, in spring and fall, and after severe weather.

Simple flat roof maintenance

  • Clear drains, scuppers and gutters, especially after leaf drop in the fall
  • Keep foot traffic to walk pads or marked paths
  • Schedule a commercial roof inspection of seams and flashing at least twice a year and after storms
  • Fix small punctures and open seams before winter freeze-thaw cycles make them bigger
